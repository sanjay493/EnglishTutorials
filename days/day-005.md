# Day 5 · Every Day Is the Same: Present Simple

## Warm-up: 5 minutes

You already know *I am a writer*. Now you need *I write*, *I start work at nine*, *I drink coffee every morning*.

The **present simple** is the tense of habits, routines, facts, and timetables. It is the most useful tense in English, and it has one famous trap: the extra **-s** for *he*, *she*, and *it*.

**Say it out loud:** "I work. You work. He work**s**. She work**s**. We work. They work."

### Today's words

| Word or phrase | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| get up | to leave your bed | I get up at seven. |
| have breakfast | to eat the first meal | She has breakfast at home. |
| commute | to travel to work | He commutes by train. |
| start work | to begin working | We start work at nine. |
| check email | to read and answer messages | I check email first thing. |
| have a meeting | to attend a meeting | She has a meeting at ten. |
| finish | to end | I finish at six. |
| go to the gym | to exercise | He goes to the gym twice a week. |
| relax | to rest and do something calm | We relax in the evening. |
| go to bed | to go to sleep | I go to bed around eleven. |

**Say it out loud:** say your own routine in order, using five of the verbs above.

## Grammar: present simple

### The rule

Use the present simple for things that are **generally true**, **happen regularly**, or are **timetabled**.

- Habits and routines: *I drink tea every morning.*
- Facts: *Water boils at 100°C.*
- Timetables: *The train leaves at 7:15.*
- Permanent situations: *She lives in Lisbon.*

The form is the **base verb** for *I, you, we, they*, and the base verb **+ s** for *he, she, it*.

### How it looks

| Subject | Form | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I / you / we / they | base verb | I **work** from home. |
| he / she / it | base verb + **-s** | She **works** from home. |
| Negative | do/does + not | He **doesn't work** on Fridays. |
| Question | do/does + subject | **Does** she **work** here? |

Negatives and questions are tomorrow's lesson. Today, build the affirmative until it is automatic.

### Spelling: the third person

| Rule | Base | He / she / it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Add **-s** | work, live, start | works, lives, starts |
| Add **-es** after ch, sh, s, x, o | watch, finish, go, do | watches, finishes, goes, does |
| Consonant + **y** → **-ies** | study, try, carry | studies, tries, carries |
| Vowel + **y** → **-s** | play, enjoy, buy | plays, enjoys, buys |
| Irregular | have | **has** |

### In real sentences

1. I **start** work at nine, and my manager **starts** at eight.
2. She **has** breakfast at home, but she **drinks** coffee at the office.
3. The shop **opens** at ten and **closes** at seven.

### Common mistakes

| Wrong | Right | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| He work in Delhi. | He **works** in Delhi. | Third person singular needs *-s*. |
| She go to the office. | She **goes** to the office. | Verbs ending in *o* take *-es*. |
| I am work in a bank. | I **work** in a bank. | *Work* is the verb. Do not add *am*. |
| It have two rooms. | It **has** two rooms. | *Have* is irregular. |
| He studys English. | He **studies** English. | Consonant + *y* → *-ies*. |
| I work every day. I am tired every day. | Same — but note **every day** (two words) vs **everyday** (adjective). | *Everyday* means ordinary: *everyday English*. |

### Watch out

The **-s** is easy to forget because it carries no meaning you can hear clearly. Slow down on *he / she / it* and say the ending on purpose: *She work-**s**.* In fast speech it can sound like /s/, /z/, or /ɪz/: *works*, *lives*, *watches*.

## Speaking lab: a daily routine interview

### Dialogue A: informal

> **Situation:** two colleagues, Sam and Ravi, talk over coffee before work.

**Sam:** What time do you usually start work?

**Ravi:** Around nine. I'm not a morning person.

**Sam:** Me neither. Do you work from home?

**Ravi:** Two days a week. I go to the office on Tuesdays and Thursdays.

**Sam:** And what do you do at lunch?

**Ravi:** I usually go for a walk. Sometimes I meet a friend.

**Sam:** Nice. I just eat at my desk.

**Ravi:** That's not a break!

**Sam:** I know. I check email at the same time.

**Ravi:** You need a hobby.

### Dialogue B: formal

> **Situation:** Ms Ito and Ana agree a time for their weekly check-in.

**Ms Ito:** Good morning, Ana. Could we agree a time for our weekly check-in?

**Ana:** Certainly. I start at eight thirty, so nine o'clock works well.

**Ms Ito:** Nine o'clock on Mondays?

**Ana:** That suits me. The meeting usually takes thirty minutes.

**Ms Ito:** Good. I'll send a calendar invitation.

**Ana:** Thank you. I'll prepare the status report beforehand.

**Ms Ito:** And does the report include the Lisbon figures?

**Ana:** It does. I update them every Friday.

### What changed?

| Informal | Formal | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What time do you usually start? | Could we agree a time for our check-in? | A request framed as a shared decision |
| Around nine. | I start at eight thirty. | Precise information in a formal context |
| Me neither. | That suits me. | Formal agreement |
| Two days a week. | The meeting usually takes thirty minutes. | Full sentence instead of a fragment |
| I'll send... / I'll prepare... | Same, but with a clear action and deadline | Professional follow-through |

### Pronunciation and delivery

- **The -s ending has three sounds.** /s/ after quiet sounds (*works*, *starts*), /z/ after loud sounds (*lives*, *goes*), /ɪz/ after s, ch, sh, x (*watches*, *finishes*).
- **usually** is /ˈjuːʒuəli/ in British English, with the *s* sounding like the *s* in *measure*.
- **Sentence stress in questions.** *What TIME do you usually START?* The question word and the main verb carry the beat.
- **Weak *do*.** In *Do you work from home?*, *do* is /də/, not /duː/.

### Your turn

Interview someone (or yourself) about a normal working day. Ask about start time, lunch, home working, and the end of the day. Use *he* or *she* in your answers, and do not forget the **-s**. Record 60 seconds.

## Writing lab: write about your day

### Model text

> I usually get up at seven and have breakfast at home. I leave the house at eight and commute by train. I start work at nine and check email first. At one, I have lunch with a colleague. In the afternoon, I have meetings or write reports. I finish at six, go to the gym twice a week, and relax in the evening. I go to bed around eleven.

### Why it works

- It uses **time markers** to create a clear sequence: *at seven*, *at eight*, *at nine*, *at one*, *in the afternoon*.
- Every verb is in the present simple, because this is a routine.
- It mixes short and longer sentences, so it does not sound like a list.
- It includes one personal detail (*twice a week*) that makes it real.

### Structure you can reuse

```text
1. Morning:    I get up at ___ and ___.
2. Travel:     I leave at ___ and commute by ___.
3. Work:       I start at ___. I usually ___.
4. Midday:     At ___, I ___.
5. Afternoon:  In the afternoon, I ___.
6. Evening:    I finish at ___. I ___ and ___.
```

### Editing checklist

- [ ] Every verb is present simple.
- [ ] Third-person verbs (if you write about someone else) have *-s*.
- [ ] Time expressions use the right preposition: *at* seven, *on* Monday, *in* the morning.
- [ ] There is at least one detail that is specific to you.
- [ ] *every day* is two words when it means "each day".
