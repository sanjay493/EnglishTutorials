# Day 3 · A Book, The Book, Books: Articles and Plurals

> **Module 1 · Foundations** · Level A1 · 45-60 minutes

## Warm-up: 5 minutes

English has three small words that cause big problems: **a**, **an**, and **the**. They carry information. *I need a pen* means any pen. *I need the pen* means a specific pen, probably the one we both know about.

Today you will also learn how to make plurals, including the irregular ones that refuse to follow the rules.

**Say it out loud:** "a book, the book, books. a child, the child, children."

### Today's words

| Word or phrase | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| pen / pencil | things you write with | I need a pen. |
| notebook | a book for writing notes | This notebook is full. |
| bag | something you carry things in | The bag is on the chair. |
| key | a small metal object that opens a lock | Where is my key? |
| card | plastic money, or a piece of thick paper | Can I pay by card? |
| receipt | proof that you paid | Could I have a receipt? |
| price | how much something costs | What's the price? |
| size | how big or small something is | Do you have a larger size? |
| pair | two things that go together | a pair of gloves |
| stationery | pens, paper, and office supplies | We need to order stationery. |

**Say it out loud:** point at five objects near you and say *a ___* or *the ___*.

## Grammar: articles and plurals

### The rule

**a / an** = one thing, not a specific one. Use it the first time you mention something, and with jobs.

**the** = a specific thing. Use it when the listener knows which one you mean, when you mention it a second time, or when there is only one.

**no article** = general statements about plural or uncountable nouns.

Choose **a** or **an** by **sound**, not by letter: *a university* (y sound), *an hour* (silent h), *an MBA* (em sound), *a European* (y sound).

### How it looks

| Use | Article | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One thing, not specific | a / an | I need **a** pen. |
| A job | a / an | She's **an** engineer. |
| Already mentioned | the | I bought a pen. **The** pen is blue. |
| Both people know which one | the | **The** meeting starts at nine. |
| Only one exists | the | **The** sun is bright. |
| General plural | — | **Books** are expensive. |
| General uncountable | — | **Water** is important. |

### Plurals

| Rule | Singular | Plural |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Add **-s** | book, key | books, keys |
| Add **-es** after ch, sh, s, x, o | watch, box, bus, tomato | watches, boxes, buses, tomatoes |
| Consonant + **y** → **-ies** | city, baby | cities, babies |
| Some **-f / -fe** → **-ves** | shelf, knife | shelves, knives |
| Irregular | man, woman, child, person, tooth, foot, mouse, sheep, fish | men, women, children, people, teeth, feet, mice, sheep, fish |

*Person* has a regular plural **persons** in legal and very formal writing ("two persons were injured"), but **people** is the normal plural everywhere else.

### In real sentences

1. I need **a** notebook and **a** pen. **The** notebook is for work.
2. She's **an** engineer. She works with **a** team of **eight people**.
3. **Children** need **paper** and **pencils**, not just screens.

### Common mistakes

| Wrong | Right | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I am engineer. | I am **an** engineer. | Jobs take *a/an* in English. |
| She is doctor. | She is **a** doctor. | Same rule, even in a formal introduction. |
| a hour | **an** hour | The *h* is silent, so the word starts with a vowel sound. |
| two childs | two **children** | Irregular plural. |
| I like the dogs. | I like **dogs**. | General statement: no article. |
| The life is beautiful. | **Life** is beautiful. | Abstract general statement: no article. |

### Watch out

**The** is not a fancy version of **a**. If you say *I'm looking for the manager*, you mean one specific manager. If you say *I'm looking for a manager*, any manager will do.

## Speaking lab: objects, prices, and spelling

### Dialogue A: informal

> **Situation:** a street market in London. Nina wants a scarf and some gloves.

**Nina:** Excuse me, how much is this scarf?

**Vendor:** Which one? The blue one or the grey one?

**Nina:** The grey one.

**Vendor:** That's twelve pounds.

**Nina:** And how much are those gloves?

**Vendor:** Eight pounds a pair.

**Nina:** OK, I'll take the scarf and a pair of gloves. Can I pay by card?

**Vendor:** Yes, of course.

**Nina:** Great. Could I have a bag, please?

**Vendor:** Here you are.

### Dialogue B: formal

> **Situation:** Ana calls a supplier to order office stationery.

**Ana:** Good morning. This is Ana Silva from Meridian. I'd like to order some stationery.

**Agent:** Certainly. Could you give me the item codes?

**Ana:** Yes. We need twenty boxes of A4 paper, code P412.

**Agent:** P as in Papa?

**Ana:** That's right. And ten packs of blue pens, code B709.

**Agent:** B as in Bravo. Anything else?

**Ana:** A box of envelopes, please. The large size.

**Agent:** Certainly. I'll send the order confirmation by email.

**Ana:** Thank you. Could you include the price list as well?

**Agent:** Of course. I'll attach it to the confirmation.

### What changed?

| Informal | Formal | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Excuse me | Good morning. This is ___ | A formal call identifies the caller first |
| How much is this? | Could you give me the item codes? | Specific information replaces pointing |
| That's twelve pounds. | Certainly. | Formal service language |
| Can I pay by card? | Could you include the price list? | *Could you* is more polite than *can I* |
| Thanks | Thank you | Full form |

### Pronunciation and delivery

- **a / an link to the next word.** *an apple* sounds like *a-napple*: /əˈnæpl/. *a pen* is /əˈpen/.
- **Plural endings have three sounds.** /s/ after a quiet sound (*books*), /z/ after a loud sound (*keys*), /ɪz/ after s, ch, sh, x (*watches*). Adding the wrong one is a common accent marker, not a grammar error.
- **Spelling aloud.** Learn the letter names, and use the radio alphabet when it matters: *P as in Papa*, *B as in Bravo*.

### Your turn

You are buying three things in a shop. Ask about the price of one item nearby (**this**) and one item further away (**that**). Then ask for **a** bag, **a** receipt, and **a** different size. Record it once informally and once as a formal phone order.

## Writing lab: a profile paragraph

### Model text

> Ana Silva is a project lead at Meridian. She manages a team of six people in Lisbon. Ana is from Brazil, and she speaks Portuguese, Spanish, and English. In her free time, she runs and takes photographs. Her colleagues describe her as calm and organised.

### Why it works

- It opens with a **topic sentence**: who the person is and what they do.
- The next sentences add role, place, background, and personality, in that order.
- Articles are doing real work: *a project lead* (one of several), *the Meridian account* (specific), *a team of six people* (one team).
- It uses **present simple**, because these are permanent or repeated facts. (You will study that tomorrow.)

### Structure you can reuse

```text
1. Name + job:      ___ is a ___ at ___.
2. Team + place:    He/She manages ___ in ___.
3. Background:      ___ is from ___, and he/she speaks ___.
4. Interests:       In his/her free time, ___.
5. One detail:      Colleagues describe him/her as ___.
```

### Editing checklist

- [ ] Jobs have *a/an*: *a designer*, *an engineer*.
- [ ] General plurals have no article: *She manages teams*, not *the teams*.
- [ ] Second mentions use *the*.
- [ ] Irregular plurals are correct: *people*, *children*, *women*, *men*.
- [ ] The paragraph has a topic sentence and one closing detail.

Write 60-80 words about a colleague, a classmate, or a fictional person. Use at least one *a/an*, one *the*, one general plural with no article, and one irregular plural.

## Answer key

### Exercise 1

1. **an** — *engineer* starts with a vowel sound.
2. **a** — one notebook, not specific.
3. **—** — general uncountable statement.
4. **an** — *honest* starts with a vowel sound; the *h* is silent.
5. **a** — one meeting, not specific yet.

### Exercise 2

1. **a / The** — first mention, then a specific pen already mentioned.
2. **The** — there is only one sun in this context.
3. **an / the** — one office, not specific; *the centre* is a specific part of Lisbon.
4. **— / —** — general statements about children and paper.
5. **a** — any bank, not a specific one.

### Exercise 3

1. **boxes** — add *-es* after *x*.
2. **cities** — consonant + *y* → *-ies*.
3. **knives** — *-fe* → *-ves*.
4. **children** — irregular.
5. **people** — irregular (the normal plural of *person*).

### Exercise 4

One possible version: *Ravi Menon is a photographer at Northlight Studio. He works with a team of four people in Bristol. Ravi is from Kerala, and he speaks Malayalam, Hindi, and English. In his free time, he takes photographs of the city. His colleagues describe him as patient and curious.*
