## Warm-up: 5 minutes

Point at something near you. Say **this**. Point at something across the room. Say **that**. Now point at two things: **these** and **those**.

Today is about location and ownership: *this bag*, *those shoes*, *my bag*, *Maya's bag*.

**Say it out loud:** "This is my pen. That is your pen. These are our books. Those are their bags."

### Today's words

| Word or phrase | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| jacket | a short coat | This jacket is warm. |
| boots | shoes that cover the ankle | Those boots are nice. |
| size | how big or small clothes are | Do you have this in a medium? |
| fit | to be the right size | It doesn't fit. |
| try on | to put clothes on to test them | Can I try this on? |
| changing room | a small room for trying clothes | The changing room is over there. |
| receipt | proof of payment | Keep your receipt. |
| belong to | to be owned by | This bag belongs to me. |
| ours / theirs | possessive pronouns | The blue car is ours. |
| broken | not working | My phone is broken. |

**Say it out loud:** choose five objects and say who owns them: *This is my ___. That is his ___.*

## Grammar: pointing and possession

### The rule

**Demonstratives** show distance and number:

| | Singular | Plural |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Near | **this** | **these** |
| Far | **that** | **those** |

**Possessive adjectives** go before a noun and never change with the noun: *my book*, *my books*.

| Subject | Possessive adjective | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | **my** | my phone |
| you | **your** | your office |
| he | **his** | his car |
| she | **her** | her bag |
| it | **its** | its colour |
| we | **our** | our team |
| they | **their** | their house |

Add **'s** to a person or animal to show possession: *Maya's phone*, *the manager's office*, *the company's policy*.

- Singular noun: add **'s** — *my brother's car*.
- Regular plural ending in *-s*: add only an apostrophe — *my parents' house*.
- Irregular plural: add **'s** — *the children's room*.
- Two owners of one thing: *Maya and Jon's project*.

### In real sentences

1. **This** jacket is too small, but **those** boots fit well.
2. **My** office is on the third floor. **Her** office is next to **mine**. (More on *mine* later.)
3. **It's Maya's phone**, not **Jon's**. **His** is on the table.

### Common mistakes

| Wrong | Right | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| This are my books. | **These are** my books. | Plural noun needs *these*. |
| These is my pen. | **This is** my pen. | Singular noun needs *this*. |
| She name is Ana. | **Her** name is Ana. | Use the possessive adjective, not the subject pronoun. |
| the name of she | **her name** | English prefers the possessive adjective here. |
| my fathers car | my **father's** car | One father, so add *'s*. |
| It's name is Meridian. | **Its** name is Meridian. | *It's* = *it is*; *its* = possessive. |
| The car of Maya is red. | **Maya's car** is red. | *Of* is possible but unnatural for people. |

### Watch out

**its** and **it's** are different words. *It's* is short for *it is* or *it has*. *Its* shows possession: *The company changed **its** logo.* If you can replace it with *it is*, write *it's*.

## Speaking lab: shopping for basics

### Dialogue A: informal

> **Situation:** a clothes shop. Nina is trying on a jacket.

**Assistant:** Hi, can I help you?

**Nina:** Yes, how much is this jacket?

**Assistant:** That one is forty pounds.

**Nina:** And how much are those boots in the window?

**Assistant:** They're sixty-five pounds. They're very popular.

**Nina:** Can I try this jacket on?

**Assistant:** Of course. The changing room is over there.

**Nina:** Thanks. It's a bit small. Do you have a larger size?

**Assistant:** Let me check. Here's a medium.

**Nina:** That's better. I'll take it.

### Dialogue B: formal

> **Situation:** an office furniture showroom. Ms Rao is choosing desks for a new office.

**Supplier:** Good afternoon. Is this your first visit to our showroom?

**Ms Rao:** Yes, it is. We're looking for furniture for a new office.

**Supplier:** Certainly. This is our modular range. These desks are adjustable.

**Ms Rao:** And what is the lead time on these?

**Supplier:** Four weeks. That model is our most popular.

**Ms Rao:** May I take this brochure?

**Supplier:** Of course. I'll also email you our price list.

**Ms Rao:** Thank you. And is delivery included in that price?

**Supplier:** Delivery is included for orders over ten units.

### What changed?

| Informal | Formal | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hi, can I help you? | Good afternoon. Is this your first visit? | Formal greeting and a softer opening |
| That one is forty pounds. | Four weeks. | Direct information, no filler |
| Can I try this on? | May I take this brochure? | *May I* is more formal than *can I* |
| Thanks. | Thank you. | Full form |
| Do you have a larger size? | Is delivery included in that price? | Full question form, no ellipsis |

### Pronunciation and delivery

- **this / these**: /ðɪs/ has a short vowel; /ðiːz/ has a long vowel and a /z/ ending. The difference matters.
- **that / those**: /ðæt/ vs /ðəʊz/ (British) or /ðoʊz/ (American).
- **Possessive 's has three sounds**: /s/ after a quiet sound (*Kate's*), /z/ after a loud sound (*Maya's*), /ɪz/ after s, ch, sh, x (*James's*).
- **Contrastive stress.** *This is **my** bag, not **yours**.* Stress the possessor.

## Writing lab: describing a photo

### Model text

> In this photo, my sister and I are at our parents' house. This is my sister, Meera, on the left. Those are her children in the garden. That's my father's old chair near the window. The house is small, but its garden is beautiful. In this picture, everyone looks happy.

### Why it works

- It starts with the **big picture** (*In this photo...*) before the details.
- **This** and **these** point to things close to the viewer; **that** and **those** point to things further away.
- Possessives do the linking work: *my sister*, *our parents' house*, *her children*, *my father's old chair*.
- It ends with a feeling, not another object, so the description has a point.

### Structure you can reuse

```text
1. Overview:     In this photo, ___ are at ___.
2. People:       This is ___. These are ___.
3. Objects:      That's ___'s ___. Those are ___.
4. Detail:       The ___ is ___, but its ___ is ___.
5. Feeling:      Everyone looks ___.
```

### Editing checklist

- [ ] *This/these* for near, *that/those* for far.
- [ ] Singular and plural demonstratives match the noun.
- [ ] Possessive adjectives match the owner, not the object.
- [ ] Apostrophe in the right place: *my parents' house*, *my father's chair*.
- [ ] *Its* (possessive) and *it's* (it is) are not confused.

## Answer key

### Exercise 1

1. **These** — plural and near.
2. **That** — singular and far.
3. **This** — singular and near.
4. **Those** — plural and far.
5. **This** — singular and near.

### Exercise 2

1. **my** — the subject is *I*.
2. **her** — the subject is *she*.
3. **our** — the subject is *we*.
4. **his** — the subject is *he*.
5. **its** — the company is a thing; possessive *its*, not *it's*.

2. **my parents' house** — regular plural, apostrophe after the *s*.
