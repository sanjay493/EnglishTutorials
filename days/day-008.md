# Day 8 · There Is a Table in the Room: Place

> **Module 1 · Foundations** · Level A1 · 45-60 minutes

## Warm-up: 5 minutes

Yesterday you asked questions: *Do you live near here?* Today you answer them with a place. *Yes, I live in a small flat near the station.*

To do that you need two things: **there is / there are**, and the small words that say **where**.

**Say it out loud:** "There is a table. There are two chairs. The table is in the room."

### Today's words

| Word or phrase | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| flat (apartment) | a home on one floor of a building | We live in a flat. |
| kitchen | the room where you cook | The kitchen is small. |
| bedroom | the room where you sleep | There are two bedrooms. |
| sofa | a long soft seat | The sofa is grey. |
| wardrobe (closet) | a tall cupboard for clothes | My clothes are in the wardrobe. |
| cupboard | a closed space with shelves | The cups are in the cupboard. |
| shelf / shelves | a flat board for books | There are three shelves. |
| lamp | a light you can move | There is a lamp here. |

**Say it out loud:** read the words twice, then cover the meaning column.

## Grammar: there is / there are and prepositions of place

### The rule

Use **there is** for one thing and **there are** for two or more. The word *there* does not mean a place here. It only starts the sentence, and the real subject comes after the verb: *There is a table in the room.*

For a negative, use **isn't / aren't**. For a question, put **is / are** first: *Is there a lift?*

### How it looks

| Form | One thing | Two or more | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Affirmative | There **is** a table. | There **are** two chairs. | *is* = one, *are* = two or more |
| Negative | There **isn't** a table. | There **aren't any** chairs. | *any* before a plural noun |
| Question | **Is there** a table? | **Are there any** chairs? | *is / are* comes first |
| Short answer | Yes, there is. | No, there aren't. | Do not repeat the noun |

### Prepositions of place

| Word | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| in | inside | The plates are in the cupboard. |
| on | on a surface | The lamp is on the table. |
| under | below | The bag is under the desk. |
| next to / beside | at the side of | The bathroom is next to my room. |
| between | in the middle of two things | The lamp is between the sofa and the door. |
| in front of | facing something | There is a garden in front of the flat. |
| behind | at the back of | The car park is behind the building. |
| opposite | facing across a street or room | The café is opposite the office. |

### In real sentences

1. There is a small desk in my bedroom. There are two shelves above it.
2. Is there a lift in your building? No, there isn't. There is a car park behind it.
3. There aren't any chairs in the meeting room. There is one in the kitchen.

### Common mistakes

| Wrong | Right | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| There is two chairs. | There **are** two chairs. | Two chairs is plural. |
| It is a table in my room. | **There is** a table in my room. | *It is* names a known thing; *there is* says a thing exists. |
| Is there any chairs? | **Are there** any chairs? | A plural noun needs *are*. |
| The lamp is in the table. | The lamp is **on** the table. | *On* = surface, *in* = inside. |
| There have two chairs. | There **are** two chairs. | *Have* needs an owner: *my room has two chairs*. |

### Watch out

*There is* says what a place contains. *Have got* says what an owner has. Both describe the same room: *The flat has got a balcony* = *There is a balcony in the flat*. The difference is the subject. After *there*, nothing owns anything.

## Speaking lab: describing your home and workplace

### Dialogue A: informal

> **Situation:** Nina has just moved into a new flat. Her colleague Tom comes to see it.

**Nina:** Come in. Sorry about the boxes.

**Tom:** No problem. This is a nice size.

**Nina:** There's a living room, a kitchen, and two bedrooms upstairs.

**Tom:** Is there a balcony?

**Nina:** No, there isn't. But there's a garden in front of the building.

**Tom:** Where's the kitchen?

**Nina:** Through there, next to the bathroom.

**Tom:** Sorry, next to what?

**Nina:** The bathroom. Just past the kitchen door.

**Tom:** Got it. I'd never leave.

### Dialogue B: formal

> **Situation:** Ms Adeyemi, a facilities manager, shows Mr Fischer an office floor his company may rent.

**Ms Adeyemi:** Good morning. Thank you for coming to see the third floor.

**Mr Fischer:** Good morning. The reception area looks well maintained.

**Ms Adeyemi:** Thank you. There is a meeting room at the end of the corridor. There are two smaller offices on the left.

**Mr Fischer:** Is there a kitchen for staff?

**Ms Adeyemi:** Yes, there is. It is opposite the lift, and there is a storage cupboard beside it.

**Mr Fischer:** And are there any windows in the smaller offices?

**Ms Adeyemi:** There are two windows in each office. They face the courtyard.

**Mr Fischer:** That would suit us. Is there parking?

**Ms Adeyemi:** There is, although it is shared with the building next door.

**Mr Fischer:** I see. Could you send the floor plan?

**Ms Adeyemi:** Certainly. I will send it this afternoon.

### What changed?

| Informal | Formal | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Come in. This is a nice size. | Thank you for coming to see the third floor. | A professional welcome, not a casual greeting |
| No, there isn't. | There is, although it is shared with the building next door. | A full answer with a polite limitation |
| Where's the kitchen? | Could you send the floor plan? | A direct question word becomes a polite request |
| Sorry, next to what? | I see. Could you...? | Informal repair vs formal acknowledgement |

### Pronunciation and delivery

- **"There is"** is one word in speech: /ðeəz/, like *thearz*. *There's a table* sounds like "thearz-a table".
- **"There are"** weakens to /ðərə/. *There are two* sounds like "th'ra two".
- **Stress the place, not the preposition.** *The lamp is ON the TAble.* In *next to*, stress NEXT.

## Writing lab: describe a room

### Model text

> **Room in a shared flat, north of the centre**
>
> There is a bright bedroom on the first floor of a quiet flat. There are two windows, one above the desk and one next to the bed. There is a wardrobe, a small bookcase, and a lamp on the desk. The bathroom is opposite the room, and the kitchen is at the end of the corridor. There is a table in the kitchen, and there are four chairs. There is a shared garden behind the building, and the station is a ten-minute walk away.

### Why it works

- It answers the reader's first question at once: what is this, and where is it?
- It moves from big to small: the room, the furniture, the flat, then the area.
- Almost every sentence starts with *there is* or *there are*, so the reader never doubts that the room exists.

### Structure you can reuse

```text
1. What it is:   There is a ___ on the ___ floor.
2. The walls:    There are ___ windows. One is above the ___.
3. Furniture:    There is a ___, a ___, and a ___.
4. Nearby rooms: The ___ is next to / opposite the ___.
5. Outside:      There is a ___ behind / in front of the building.
6. Location:     The ___ is a ___-minute walk away.
```

### Editing checklist

- [ ] *There is* with one thing; *there are* with two or more.
- [ ] Every place word has a noun after it: *on the desk*, not *on desk*.
- [ ] No sentence starts with *It is* when you mean *There is*.
- [ ] The description goes in a clear order, not randomly.

## Answer key

### Exercise 1

1. **is** — *a lamp* is one thing.
2. **are** — *two windows* is plural.
3. **Is** — the noun is singular, so the question starts with *is*.
4. **aren't** — *chairs* is plural, and the sentence is negative.
5. **Are** — *shops* is plural.

### Exercise 2

1. **in** — the plates are inside the cupboard.
2. **between** — one table, two chairs.
3. **on** — we say *on the third floor*.
4. **next to / opposite** — either is correct if it is true in your home.
5. **behind / in front of** — the preposition depends on where the garden is.

### Exercise 3

1. **There is a table in the room.** — *there is* + singular noun + place.
2. **There are two bedrooms in the flat.** — the owner becomes the place.
3. **No, there aren't any chairs.** — plural noun, negative short answer.

### Exercise 4

One possible version: *There is a small living room in my flat. There are two windows, one behind the sofa and one next to the door. There is a bookshelf between the windows, and there is a lamp on a low table. The kitchen is opposite the living room. There is a round table in the kitchen, and there are three chairs. My bicycle is behind the door.*
