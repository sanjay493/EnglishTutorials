# Start here

You do not need to read 120 lessons to begin. You need one lesson, today, and a way to come back tomorrow.

## 1. Do this in the next 20 minutes

1. Open **[Day 1](days/day-001.md)** and read it once without writing anything.
2. Do Exercise 1 only.
3. Say the two dialogues out loud, once slowly and once at normal speed.
4. Write the writing task in a notes app.
5. Check your answers. Note the number you got right.

That is the whole habit. Everything else is repetition.

## 2. What you need

| Thing | Why |
| --- | --- |
| A notebook or notes app | Exercises, new words, and your error log |
| A phone recorder | Speaking labs are only useful out loud |
| 45-60 minutes a day | Split it: 20 minutes in the morning, 30 in the evening works |
| This site, bookmarked | [PROGRESS.md](PROGRESS.md) tells you where you are |

No app, subscription, or tutor is required. A tutor or a language partner makes speaking faster, but the course is designed to work alone.

## 3. The daily routine

| Step | Time | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Warm-up | 5 min | Read yesterday's *Check yourself* table. Say the new words aloud. |
| 2. Grammar | 15 min | Read the rule, copy the form table by hand, then cover the examples and rebuild them. |
| 3. Speaking | 15 min | Read Dialogue A aloud. Then Dialogue B. Record yourself. Compare. |
| 4. Writing | 15 min | Write the task without stopping. Then edit with the checklist. |
| 5. Practice | 10 min | Do the exercises, mark them, and log every mistake in one place. |
| 6. Review | 5 min | Add 5 words to your vocabulary log. Preview tomorrow. |

**The 24-hour rule:** if you miss a day, do not do two lessons to catch up. Do the next lesson and move on. Consistency beats volume.

## 5. How to practise speaking alone

1. **Shadowing.** Play a line from the lesson, pause, and repeat it immediately, copying the rhythm, not just the words.
2. **Record and compare.** Record the same dialogue twice, one week apart. Listen for one thing only: sentence stress.
3. **Role-play both sides.** Read Maya's lines, then Jon's. The turn-taking is the skill.
4. **The 60-second rule.** After each lesson, talk for 60 seconds about the topic without notes. Restart once if you freeze, then let it be imperfect.
5. **Repair phrases are progress.** Saying *Sorry, could you repeat that?* fluently is better than staying silent.

## 6. How to practise writing alone

1. Write first, edit second. Never both at once.
2. Use the lesson checklist, then one extra pass for **verbs**: tense, agreement, form.
3. Keep every piece. On assessment days you will revise an old text; the improvement is the proof.
4. Read your text aloud. If you run out of breath, the sentence is too long.
5. Steal structures, not sentences. The *Structure you can reuse* box is there for that.

## 7. Track your own progress

- **Lessons:** tick them off in [PROGRESS.md](PROGRESS.md).
- **Errors:** keep one page titled "Things I keep getting wrong". Review it every Sunday.
- **Words:** 5 a day is 600 by Day 120. Review with spaced repetition on Days 7, 14, 30, 60, 90, and 120.
- **Speaking:** record a 60-second monologue every Sunday. Same topic, four weeks in a row, at the start of each module.
- **Writing:** keep a word count. By Day 120 you should have written about 15,000 words.

## 8. If you get stuck

| Problem | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| "I understand the rule but forget it when speaking" | That is normal. Do the speaking lab twice, then use the structure in a real message today. |
| "There are too many words" | Learn the example sentences, not the word list. One good sentence beats ten definitions. |
| "I don't have anyone to talk to" | Record yourself. Then use the *Your turn* task as a message to a friend or a post in a learning community. |
| "I missed a week" | Restart at the next lesson. Do not restart the course. |
| "This lesson is too easy" | Do the writing task with a stricter constraint: half the words, or a formal register. |
| "This lesson is too hard" | Read the grammar section twice, do Exercises 1-2 only, and come back to the rest tomorrow. |

## 9. The one promise

Do 20 minutes today. The course is 120 days long because fluency is built by returning, not by cramming.
