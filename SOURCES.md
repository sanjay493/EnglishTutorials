# Sources and further reading

This course is original writing, but its **scope and sequence**, its **accuracy checks**, and its **style advice** are informed by the reference works and teaching resources below. Nothing here is copied into the lessons; where a lesson uses a source directly, it is credited in that lesson's *Go deeper* section.

**A note on trust:** grammar references and style guides are the backbone. Social media and YouTube channels are used for **how people actually speak now**, not as authorities on rules. Anything that changes fast (slang, corporate jargon) is dated in the lesson.

## 9. How the 120 days were sequenced

The order follows three rules:

1. **Frequency first.** High-frequency structures (`be`, present simple, past simple, articles) come before low-frequency ones (subjunctive, inversion).
2. **Contrast later.** A form is introduced alone, then contrasted with its nearest neighbour two or three lessons later (present perfect on Day 27, contrasted with past simple on Day 31).
3. **Use immediately.** Every grammar point is used in the same lesson's speaking and writing labs. Nothing is learned "for later".

The CEFR descriptors and the A1-C1 topic lists from Cambridge, the British Council, and Tracktest informed the module boundaries. The business module follows the functional syllabus used in BEC and in *Business Vocabulary in Use*.

## 10. Adding a source

When you add a source to a lesson:

1. Confirm the link works and is not paywalled for the core claim.
2. Add it to this file under the right heading, with one line on what it informs.
3. Prefer primary references (a dictionary, a style guide, a corpus) over aggregator blogs.
4. For anything time-sensitive (slang, trends), put the year in the lesson.
5. Never cite a source you have not opened.
